Before you start

What you need

  • Claude Code (claude.com/claude-code) — runs the whole job from one prompt (needs a paid Claude plan)
  • yt-dlp (github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp) — downloads the stream's audio, its chat replay and the clip parts
  • ffmpeg (ffmpeg.org) — measures the loudness and cuts the clips
  • Python with faster-whisper (captions) and Pillow (drawing the text)

On a Mac, install them in Terminal:

Copy this prompt
brew install yt-dlp ffmpeg python
pip3 install faster-whisper pillow
No libass? No problem

Many ffmpeg builds can't burn subtitles. The prompt draws the captions as images with Pillow and lays them on top, so any ffmpeg works.

Step 1

Pick a streamer who pays

Only clip streamers who run a clipping program. They want your posts, they pay per view, and you're allowed to use their streams. Live campaigns are listed on Content Rewards, a clipping campaign marketplace; payouts run through Whop. Many streamers also post their own program in their Discord or bio.

$1.09median paid per 1,000 views (707 campaigns, Content Rewards, 10 Nov 2026)
$1.4MN3on paid 303 clippers in five weeks (Business Insider)
Read the program's rules first

Each program says which platforms pay, the rate, how long a clip must be and how to tag it. Rates are listed rates, not what you'll earn, and a 10% platform fee is common. Always credit the streamer in the clip and the caption.

Step 2

The prompt

Paste into Claude Code in an empty folder. Swap {URL} and {CREDIT}.

Copy this prompt
Turn this stream into vertical clips: {URL}

Work in this folder. Use yt-dlp, ffmpeg and Python (faster-whisper, Pillow).

1. Download the stream's audio only (yt-dlp -f "ba[abr<=70]/ba") and, at the same time in the background, its chat replay (yt-dlp --skip-download --write-subs --sub-langs live_chat). If the chat replay isn't there or isn't finished when the audio is measured, go on with the audio alone and say so.
2. Find the 10 loudest moments. Measure loudness every second (ffmpeg ebur128, short-term). A moment scores by how far it jumps above the median of the 5 minutes around it, plus how much the chat speeds up in the same 10 seconds (messages per 10 s vs the stream's median). Keep moments at least 3 minutes apart and skip the first and last 5 minutes.
3. Each clip runs from 10 s before the peak to 20 s after (30 s). Download only those parts in 1080p: yt-dlp --download-sections "*START-END" --force-keyframes-at-cuts -f "bv*[height<=1080]+ba/b".
4. Make each clip 1080x1920: the stream frame scaled to full width in the middle, a blurred, darker copy of it filling the background.
5. Transcribe each clip with faster-whisper (the "small" model, word timestamps), one clip at a time, saving each transcript as it finishes. Captions: 2-3 words at a time, bold white with a black outline, in the lower third. Draw them with Pillow as transparent PNGs and overlay them with ffmpeg, so no libass is needed.
6. Write a title for each clip, at most 6 words, from what is said, in capitals, in the top band. Under it a small credit line: "{CREDIT}".
7. Save clips/01.mp4 ... clips/10.mp4, a cover still for each (the frame at the peak), and moments.csv: rank, time in the stream, loudness jump (LU), chat speed (x), title. Then print that table.

Run every step in the foreground, each command under 10 minutes. Don't stop until all 10 clips and moments.csv exist.
Step 3

What Claude does

In order
1Downloads the audio only — a 10-hour stream is a few hundred MB, not 30 GB
2Gets the chat replay — chat speeding up means something happened
3Finds the loudest moments — loudness every second vs the 5 minutes around it
4Downloads only those parts in 1080p — 30 s each
5Cuts them to 9:16 — the stream in the middle, a blurred copy behind
6Adds captions and a title — 2–3 words at a time, title from what's said
My run on Speed's GTA 6 stream

One prompt, 11 minutes, no edits by me: 9 hours of audio measured, 5 cutscenes skipped, the 10 loudest moments cut to 1080×1920 with captions and titles (18 Nov 2026, Speed's live stream on YouTube).

Stream still live?

yt-dlp can't cut parts out of a live YouTube stream. Tell Claude the stream is live: it can download the audio aired so far and only the 2-second pieces around each moment. Or wait for the replay, then the prompt works as written.

Step 4

Check before you post

  • Watch every clip. Loud isn't always good: drop a scream with no story.
  • Skip anything Instagram would hide. Two of my ten were a shower scene; I left them out.
  • No cutscenes or story spoilers. Game studios take those down; keep the streamer's own reactions.
  • Credit the streamer on screen and in the caption, and use the program's tag or link.
  • No plain reposts. Your captions and title make it an original edit.
  • Post where the program pays and submit the link the way it asks.

Now clip your first stream
